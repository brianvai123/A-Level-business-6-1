External Influences on Business Activity IGCSE Business · Topic 6.1 · Grade 12

Why this matters: No business operates in a vacuum. External factors — political, legal, economic, social, technological, competitive, international, and environmental — shape what businesses can do, how much it costs, and how successful they are. Understanding these influences is essential for making informed business decisions.

6.1.1 Political and Legal Influences

1 Privatisation vs Nationalisation

DEFINITION

Privatisation

Transferring ownership of a business or industry from the public sector (government) to the private sector.

✔ Advantages
  • Increases efficiency (profit motive)
  • Raises government revenue
  • Reduces taxpayer burden
  • Encourages competition & innovation
  • Access to private capital
✘ Disadvantages
  • May lead to job losses
  • Risk of monopoly / higher prices
  • Essential services may be cut
  • Loss of public control
  • Wealth concentrated in few hands
DEFINITION

Nationalisation

Transferring ownership of a business or industry from the private sector to the public sector (government).

✔ Advantages
  • Protects essential services
  • Prevents monopoly exploitation
  • Government controls strategic industries
  • Can ensure universal access
  • Profits go to the public
✘ Disadvantages
  • Often less efficient (no profit motive)
  • Costly for taxpayers
  • Political interference in decisions
  • May reduce innovation
  • Bureaucracy & slow decision-making
Exam tip: When asked to recommend privatisation or nationalisation, always weigh up the specific situation — for example, a loss-making state airline (privatise) vs a natural monopoly like water supply (nationalise).

2 How government uses law to control business

Area of law What it controls Impact on business
Employment practices Contracts, discrimination, unfair dismissal, trade unions, minimum working age Must follow fair recruitment; may face tribunals; increased HR costs
Conditions of work (incl. health & safety) Working hours, rest breaks, protective equipment, workplace safety Higher compliance costs; fewer accidents; better productivity
Wage levels National minimum wage / living wage Higher labour costs; may reduce staff or raise prices
Marketing behaviour Advertising standards, product labelling, misleading claims, selling to minors Must be truthful; restrictions on certain ads (tobacco, alcohol)
Competition Anti-competitive practices, monopolies, cartels, price-fixing Cannot collude; may block mergers; fines for breaches
Location decisions Planning permission, zoning laws, environmental impact Cannot build anywhere; may need public consultation; delays
Particular goods & services Bans or restrictions (e.g. drugs, weapons, gambling, sugary drinks) Cannot produce/sell certain items; may need licences

📋 Scenario: Government bans advertising of sugary drinks to children

A soft drink manufacturer must change its marketing strategy. It now focuses on adult consumers, uses healthier recipes, and re-brands some products.

Impact: Short-term sales may fall; long-term reputation improves; R&D costs rise.

6.1.2 Economic Influences

1 How government intervenes in the economy

HELPING BUSINESS

Interventions to encourage enterprise

  • Grants & subsidies — money to start or expand
  • Tax breaks — lower corporation tax, R&D credits
  • Deregulation — fewer rules to reduce costs
  • Enterprise zones — low tax, simplified planning
  • Training schemes — funded apprenticeships
  • Loans & loan guarantees — help SMEs access finance
  • Infrastructure investment — roads, broadband
CONSTRAINING BUSINESS

Interventions to restrict activity

  • Higher taxes — reduces profit & spending
  • Stricter regulation — compliance costs rise
  • Price controls — e.g. caps on rent or energy
  • Import tariffs / quotas — raise trade costs
  • Nationalisation — government takeover
  • Anti-monopoly laws — block mergers
MARKET FAILURE

How governments deal with market failure

Market failure occurs when the free market fails to allocate resources efficiently. Examples: pollution, monopolies, public goods, information gaps.

  • Taxes on negative externalities (e.g. carbon tax)
  • Subsidies for positive externalities (e.g. education, healthcare)
  • Provision of public goods (defence, street lighting)
  • Regulation (e.g. pollution limits)
  • Competition policy (break up monopolies)
  • Information campaigns (e.g. health warnings)

2 Key macroeconomic objectives

Objective What it means Impact on business
Low unemployment As many people working as possible Higher consumer spending; harder to recruit; wages may rise
Low inflation Prices rising slowly (target ~2%) Predictable costs; stable pricing; confidence to invest
Economic growth Rising GDP over time More demand; opportunities to expand; higher profits
Trade-offs: Governments cannot always achieve all objectives at once. For example, cutting unemployment may cause inflation; slowing inflation may reduce growth.

3 Government policies & their impact on business

Policy How it works Business impact
Monetary policy Central bank controls interest rates & money supply Lower rates → cheaper borrowing → more investment. Higher rates → less spending.
Fiscal policy Government spending & taxation Higher spending → more demand. Higher tax → less profit & spending.
Supply-side policy Improving productivity & efficiency (education, infrastructure, deregulation) Lower costs, better skills, more innovation.
Exchange rate policy Influencing value of currency Weak currency → exports cheaper, imports dearer. Strong currency → opposite.

6.1.3 Social and Demographic Influences

1 Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)

CONCEPT

What is CSR?

CSR is the idea that businesses should act in ways that benefit society, not just maximise profit. It goes beyond legal requirements.

  • Ethical accounting practices — transparent reporting, no fraud
  • Fair incentive schemes — no bribes for contracts
  • Social auditing — measuring social & environmental impact
  • Fair treatment of workers — fair pay, safe conditions
  • Community involvement — charity, local projects
ISSUES

CSR — Advantages vs Disadvantages

✔ Advantages
  • Better reputation
  • Customer loyalty
  • Motivated staff
  • Attracts ethical investors
✘ Disadvantages
  • Higher costs
  • May reduce short-term profit
  • Risk of "greenwashing"
  • Hard to measure impact
COMMUNITY & PRESSURE GROUPS

Why businesses must consider the community

  • Pressure groups — e.g. Greenpeace, Fairtrade can damage reputation
  • Local community — jobs, pollution, traffic, noise
  • Media scrutiny — bad news spreads fast
  • Customer expectations — consumers increasingly buy ethically
  • Government relations — need goodwill for permits

2 Demographic changes

Level Examples of change Business impact
Local Population growth/decline, migration, age profile Changes in demand for housing, schools, shops, healthcare
National Ageing population, falling birth rate, urbanisation More demand for healthcare & pensions; fewer workers; new products
Global Population growth in developing countries, global migration New markets; different consumer needs; global talent pool

📋 Scenario: Ageing population in a developed country

Business opportunities: Healthcare, retirement homes, mobility products, leisure for seniors.

Business challenges: Shrinking workforce, higher labour costs, pension liabilities.

6.1.4 Technological Influences

IMPACT

How technology changes business

  • Production: Automation, robotics, 3D printing → lower costs, higher output
  • Communication: Email, video calls, cloud → faster decisions, remote work
  • Marketing: Social media, big data, AI → targeted ads, personalisation
  • Distribution: E-commerce, drones → wider reach, faster delivery
  • Finance: Online banking, crypto → new payment methods
  • Customer service: Chatbots, AI → 24/7 support
CHALLENGES

Issues with technological change

  • High investment cost — equipment, training, software
  • Job displacement — workers replaced by machines
  • Cyber security risks — data breaches, hacking
  • Rapid obsolescence — tech becomes outdated quickly
  • Skills gap — need for digitally skilled workers
  • Dependence on tech — system failures can halt operations

6.1.5 Competitors and Suppliers

⚔️ Impact of competitors

  • Price competition — may force prices down, cutting margins
  • Product innovation — must keep improving to stay relevant
  • Marketing battles — higher ad spend needed
  • Customer loyalty — harder to retain if rivals offer better value
  • Market share — new entrants can erode position

🔗 Impact of suppliers

  • Prices — supplier raises prices → business costs rise
  • Reliability — late deliveries disrupt production
  • Quality — poor inputs damage final product
  • Power balance — few suppliers = supplier power
  • Ethics — supplier's practices affect buyer's reputation
Strategy: Businesses may respond to competition by differentiating, cutting costs, or forming strategic alliances. They may manage suppliers by using multiple sources, long-term contracts, or vertical integration.

6.1.6 International Influences

1 International trading links & agreements

🌍 Importance of trade links

  • Access to larger markets → more sales
  • Access to raw materials & components
  • Specialisation → efficiency gains
  • Foreign exchange earnings
  • Technology transfer & innovation

📜 Trade agreements

  • Free trade areas — no tariffs between members (e.g. NAFTA/USMCA)
  • Customs unions — common external tariff (e.g. EU)
  • Single markets — free movement of goods, services, capital, labour
  • Bilateral agreements — between two countries

Impact: Lower trade barriers → more competition, cheaper imports, but domestic firms may struggle.

💻 Role of technology in trade

  • E-commerce platforms (Amazon, Alibaba) → global reach
  • Digital payments → easier cross-border transactions
  • Logistics software → efficient shipping
  • Video conferencing → global business meetings
  • Blockchain → secure trade documentation

2 Multinational corporations (MNCs)

Advantages to host country Disadvantages to host country
Creates jobs May exploit cheap labour
Brings investment & technology Profits often sent back home
Boosts exports & tax revenue May dominate local market
Improves skills & infrastructure Can damage local competitors
Raises standards (sometimes) Environmental damage possible

📋 MNC & government relationship

Governments may court MNCs with tax breaks and grants, but may also regulate them to protect local jobs, the environment, and consumers. Tensions arise over tax avoidance, labour conditions, and profit repatriation.

6.1.7 Environmental Influences

PHYSICAL ENVIRONMENT

How environmental issues influence business

  • Climate change — carbon taxes, extreme weather disruptions
  • Pollution — fines, clean-up costs, reputation damage
  • Resource depletion — rising raw material costs
  • Waste regulations — must recycle, reduce packaging
  • Consumer pressure — demand for eco-friendly products
ENVIRONMENTAL AUDIT

What is an environmental audit?

A systematic review of a business's environmental impact — energy use, waste, emissions, water, supply chain.

Stakeholders who use it:

  • Business itself — to find cost savings & improve efficiency
  • Investors — to assess environmental risk
  • Government — to check compliance
  • Pressure groups — to hold business accountable
  • Customers — to make informed choices
SUSTAINABILITY

Why sustainability matters

  • Long-term viability — resources won't last forever
  • Brand image — green credentials attract customers
  • Cost savings — energy efficiency, less waste
  • Legal compliance — avoiding fines
  • Investor appeal — ESG investing is growing
  • Competitive edge — differentiation through sustainability

Businesses may adopt: renewable energy, circular economy models, sustainable sourcing, and carbon offsetting.
