4 Short Answer Questions

4.1 Explain two ways a government might intervene to help businesses and encourage enterprise. (4 marks)

Model answer:
  • Grants and subsidies: Governments can provide money to new or expanding businesses, reducing start-up costs and encouraging entrepreneurship.
  • Tax breaks: Lower corporation tax or R&D tax credits increase retained profit, giving businesses more funds to invest and grow.
  • (Other acceptable: enterprise zones, deregulation, training schemes, loan guarantees, infrastructure investment.)

4.2 Explain how a rise in interest rates might affect a manufacturing business. (4 marks)

Model answer:
  • Higher borrowing costs: If the business has loans or wants to invest in new machinery, the cost of finance rises, possibly delaying or cancelling investment.
  • Lower consumer demand: Consumers with mortgages pay more, so they spend less — demand for manufactured goods may fall.
  • Stronger currency (possible): Higher rates may attract foreign capital, raising the exchange rate — exports become more expensive and less competitive.

4.3 Explain three ways technological change can impact a business. (6 marks)

Model answer:
  • Production efficiency: Automation and robotics can lower unit costs and increase output, improving competitiveness.
  • Marketing reach: Social media and big data allow targeted advertising, reaching more customers at lower cost.
  • New risks: Greater reliance on technology creates cyber security risks and requires ongoing investment in training and upgrades.
  • (Other acceptable: e-commerce, remote working, AI customer service, faster communication.)

4.4 Explain why a business might conduct an environmental audit and who might use it. (6 marks)

Model answer:
  • Identify cost savings: An audit reveals areas of high energy or water use, allowing the business to reduce waste and cut costs.
  • Ensure compliance: Helps the business meet environmental regulations and avoid fines.
  • Improve reputation: Demonstrates commitment to sustainability, which can attract customers and investors.
  • Users: The business itself, investors (assessing risk), government (checking compliance), pressure groups (holding business accountable), and customers (making informed choices).

5 Case Study — Recommend & Justify

🏭 "PowerGen" — state-owned electricity company

PowerGen is a state-owned electricity company in Country Z. It has been making losses for years, relies on government subsidies, and has been criticised for poor service and slow modernisation. The government is considering privatising PowerGen.

However, some argue that electricity is an essential service and should remain under public control to protect consumers from price rises.

5.1 Recommend and justify whether the government should privatise PowerGen. (8 marks)

Model answer:

Recommendation: Yes, privatise PowerGen.

Justification:

  • Efficiency: Private ownership introduces the profit motive, encouraging cost control and better service — addressing the current losses and poor performance.
  • Investment: The private sector can raise capital for modernisation without relying on taxpayers.
  • Government revenue: Selling PowerGen raises funds that could be spent on health or education.
  • Counter-argument: Electricity is essential, so privatisation could lead to higher prices. However, this can be managed through a regulator setting price caps.
  • Conclusion: On balance, privatisation is likely to improve efficiency and service, provided a strong regulator protects consumers.

5.2 Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of nationalising a loss-making private railway company. (8 marks)

Model answer:

Advantages of nationalisation:

  • Protects an essential service — railways can continue running even if unprofitable.
  • Government can invest in infrastructure without needing short-term profit.
  • Fares can be kept affordable for passengers.
  • Profits (if any) go to the public rather than shareholders.

Disadvantages of nationalisation:

  • Taxpayers bear the cost of losses and investment.
  • State-owned firms can be less efficient due to lack of competition.
  • Political interference may affect decisions.
  • May reduce innovation and customer focus.

Conclusion: Nationalisation may be justified if the railway is essential and private ownership has failed, but the government must ensure efficiency through clear targets and accountability.

5.3 A government wants to reduce youth unemployment. Recommend and justify two policies it could use, and explain the impact on businesses. (8 marks)

Model answer:

Recommendation 1: Subsidise apprenticeships and training schemes.

  • Justification: Young people gain skills, making them more employable. Businesses receive funding to offset training costs.
  • Impact on business: Lower recruitment and training costs; access to a skilled workforce; possible long-term loyalty from trained staff.

Recommendation 2: Cut taxes for businesses that hire young workers.

  • Justification: Reduces the cost of hiring, encouraging firms to take on more young employees.
  • Impact on business: Lower labour costs; higher profits; may encourage expansion and further hiring.

Conclusion: A combination of supply-side policies (training) and fiscal incentives (tax cuts) is likely to be most effective, benefiting both young people and businesses.
